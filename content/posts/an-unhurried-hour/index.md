Suppose an hour passes without a task being finished. There is a walk, a change
in the weather, a conversation that wanders. Nothing that belongs on a progress
report.

It is tempting to rescue that hour by calling it productive in some indirect
way. Perhaps it improves concentration. Perhaps the walk will produce an idea.

But maybe an hour is allowed to be good without producing something else.

There are details that only become visible when nobody is hurrying: the shape
of a familiar tree, a neighbor's new garden, the way the light changes along the
same stretch of pavement.

Attention is part of a life, too.
