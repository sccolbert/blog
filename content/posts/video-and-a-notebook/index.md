A video can move faster than an idea settles. A notebook gives the interesting
parts somewhere to wait.

This sample uses a YouTube developer video as a stand-in while we work on the
page layout. The notes describe a way of watching; they are not a review of the
video's claims.

## Leave space for a question

This is where the running commentary goes. The player stays beside the writing
on a wide screen, so a reader can move between watching and reading naturally.

Things to capture along the way:

- The idea worth trying for yourself.
- The assumption you want to examine.
- The question that remains after the explanation.

### After watching

Try explaining one idea without replaying the video. Notice where the explanation
gets fuzzy. That is probably the useful place to return to.
{{% /video-commentary %}}

## From watching to doing

Understanding often improves when an idea has to survive a small experiment.
Make a rough version. Change one thing. Write down what happened before deciding
what it means.

A page like this can hold both halves of that process: the thing that prompted
the question, and the work of thinking it through.
